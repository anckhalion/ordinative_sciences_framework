# TE_AUDIT v0.1 — Organizational Analysis and Documentary Audit

**Version**: 0.1
**Date**: 2026-09-17
**Status**: Pre-pilot; governed by TE_CASEWORK and the TE stack.

## 1. Invocation

Trigger on a comprehensive review of company/organization records, including an unstructured pile of documents. Audit is part of that mandate, not conditional on already finding suspicious facts. Narrow tasks such as summarizing one invoice do not silently expand into a company audit. Explicit narrower scope remains narrower and is reported.

The sequence is mandate/access → SVP and first documentary wave → relevance decisions → domain analysis → mandatory systematic audit → counterverification → bounded report. Provisional urgent issues may be flagged to the authorized case owner throughout. A second analytical pass must occur even when the first analysis is reassuring.

## 2. First wave and analysis

Apply CASEWORK §§2–4. Identify versions, potential duplicates, dates, parties, obligations, authorizations, transactions, communications and missing expected material. Build a provisional chronology and map of roles, processes and resources. Do not let the first persuasive narrative determine document selection.

After the wave, explain all exclusions; preserve excluded originals. Maintain uncertainty for inaccessible items. Use LEXX for contractual/policy/delegation questions, SCIMS for organizational functioning, VERI for effects and OBSERVER to integrate. Do not infer a person's motives from a structural vector.

## 3. Mandatory risk/control coverage

For each baseline area, record a procedure, source population, actual tested scope, evidence and result, or a reason it cannot be tested/is inapplicable:

| Area | Example questions, not presumptions |
|---|---|
| Source integrity | Conflicting versions, gaps, altered narratives, authenticity limits? |
| Finance | Do invoicing, settlement and ledger records reconcile? Duplicate documents versus actual duplicate payments? |
| Procurement | Services/deliveries supported? Exceptions, splitting patterns or conflicts requiring verification? |
| Authorizations | Who could approve, execute and review? Unsupported overrides or self-approval? |
| Governance | Delegations, related-party disclosures and decisions consistent with the applicable instruments? |
| People and internal abuse | Documentary indications of coercion, retaliation, discriminatory practices or improper use of position? Alternative explanations and affected-party evidence? |
| Assets and resources | Are custody, use and transfers supported? Possible diversion versus ordinary accounting error? |
| Information and reporting | Access, changes, reporting chains, missing logs and unsupported external/internal representations? |

Extend these areas for the actual organization. Documentary coverage is not operational control testing, and full acquisition is not full substantive testing. Record sampling and untouched populations. A `not_testable` area is a limitation, not a passed control.

## 4. Findings and escalation

Use the common finding schema. Investigate indicators of abuse, fraud, corruption and other possible violations when evidenced. Never require an illicit finding as a successful outcome. Verify actor identity, acts, authority and potential benefit independently; surname matches, relationships and opportunity are not guilt.

Look for both intentional and emergent causes. Two payments require transaction evidence, not two invoice copies. Missing minutes do not establish intentional concealment. A control can be weak without a loss having occurred; a formally correct document can coexist with unsupported real execution.

An audit can recommend targeted investigation and evidence preservation to the authorized owner. It does not authorize system access, witness contact, sanctions, external allegations or notification to authorities. Those steps need the appropriate mandate and competent determination of any applicable duties. Do not contact a potentially implicated person automatically.

## 5. Closure

Deliver a linked analytical report and audit ledger, coverage/limitations, gap register, exclusion-reconsideration record, alternative explanations, unresolved matters and proposed checks. Reassess excluded documents before closure. Significant findings require review distinct from their author. Reopening produces a new case revision and invalidates old review receipts.

Use bounded statements: no exception identified in specified tests; exception supported at a specified confidence; unresolved; not testable. Never equate zero findings with absence of wrongdoing or incomplete evidence with a global clean opinion.

External professional reference: [IIA, Internal Auditing and Fraud, third edition](https://www.theiia.org/en/content/guidance/recommended/supplemental/practice-guides/global-practice-guide-internal-auditing-and-fraud), public overview consulted 2026-09-17. It identifies organizational fraud-risk assessment as an audit concern. This TE protocol does not claim IIA compliance, certification or reproduction of its full guidance.
