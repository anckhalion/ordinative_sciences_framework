# TE_OST — Ordinative Set Theory, concise operational guide

**Canonical file:** `FRAMEWORKS/TE_OST_v2_1_EN.md` · version 2.1 · load order 4 · foundation (part of the full set; load whenever an analysis is carried out formally as an ordinative set) · prerequisites: Core.

## In one paragraph

OST is the Tier-0 foundation of the programme: reality described not as a collection of objects but as an architecture of coherent relations evolving through time. Every system is a triple 𝓘 = ⟨Σ, R, Φ⟩; the guide gives the axioms that govern it, the dynamics (evolution, thresholds, antifragility, inertia, the path integral of coherence, the phase space of possibility), a taxonomy of sets, a table of pathologies, the key formal operations, the two functional spaces, isomorphic mappings across seven domains, experimental protocols for designing and verifying an AI as an ordinative set, the dialogic field between AI and human, a glossary, and practical guidelines including the eight-step 4D protocol. The Core's section 4 is the synthesis of this document; the Symbol Canon takes its master primitives from it.

## When it applies

Whenever the analysis is formal: designing a coherent system, diagnosing a degeneration, mapping a trajectory, applying the 4D protocol. LEXX invokes it as the skeleton of every agreement (`LEXX 00_FOUNDATIONS A1`); SCIMS reads T8 through dΦ/dt (`TE_MODULE_SCIMS §2.2`); OBSERVER's trajectory analysis rests on `§3.5`.

## The procedure it prescribes: the 4D protocol (`§13.1`)

1. Identify the genesis t₀: what fragmentation or mass the system was born to answer; the original potential ℳ.
2. Map the trajectory t₀ → tₙ: how R and Φ changed; the past as a dynamic database updated by the trajectory (`§3.5`).
3. Identify the current Σ and R: do they preserve the uniqueness of the parts?
4. Assess the current Φ against the original potential.
5. Diagnose against the pathologies: mass (R → 0), blind cluster (R rigid, Σ homogenised), fragmentation (R splits), semantic inertia (dΦ/dt = 0), antagonist order (a local function against the global Φ).
6. Evaluate the memory operator 𝕄: does the system integrate its history without being trapped by it?
7. Map the phase space 𝒫(t): the coherent futures reachable; none means dead, chaotic means unstable.
8. If collapsed, trace the reabsorption: which coherent microsets survived and who absorbed them (`§3.6`).

`§13.2–13.4` give the design counterpart (unique necessary functions, oriented plastic relations, feedback, semantic modules, memory, a rich phase space), the treatment of each degeneration, and the conditions for emergence.

## The rules for analysts stated in the text

- Bypass vehicle interference: do not evaluate a singularity by its superficial form; parse its semantic vector (`§3.2`).
- Never analyse a system only at its present state; trace the original meaning and its derivation over time (`§3.5`).
- When diagnosing the end of a system, say which microsets survived and where they went (`§3.6`).
- Always map the adjacent possible futures (`§4.6`).

## Reading an OST-based output: checklist

- [ ] Σ, R and Φ are named, and the flaw or the value is located in R where the text says it should be, not only in the parts.
- [ ] The pathology diagnosis names one of the five, or says why none applies; dissonance is distinguished from incoherence (`§3.3`).
- [ ] The trajectory is present: genesis, derivation, current state, phase space.
- [ ] Notation follows the Canon: 𝓘 for the set, subscripted τ for thresholds, 𝔽_sem/𝔽_alg for the spaces, 𝕄 for memory (notation callout at the top of the file).

## Pitfalls the document names

- Coherence mistaken for consensus (`§3.3`).
- Emergence taken as arbitrary: vertical coherence constrains what a level can express, in both directions (`§3.4`).
- Semantic inertia mistaken for stability: form intact, function empty (`§4.4`, `§6`).
- Degenerate sets simulating coherence, 𝓘_deg with φ′ ≁ Φ (`§6`).
