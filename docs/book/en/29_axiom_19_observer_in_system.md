# Chapter 29 — The observer is part of the system

## The question that remains open

Does the neutral observer exist? Does there exist, that is, a position from which a subject can observe a system without altering it, register the facts without co-determining them, evaluate truth without being part of what one evaluates? The classical answer, which has shaped much of modern science and the underlying naive epistemology, is affirmative. The observer is conceived as a *gaze* settling on the world: the world happens, and the observer registers it. The validity of what is registered depends on the *neutrality* of the observer — the more neutral, the more "objective" the registration. The ideal of the scientist as disinterested spectator, of the historian as impartial narrator, of the judge as arbiter above the parties, of the philosopher as pure thinker, share this structure: the observer is outside, the system is inside, and between the two there is a sharp boundary.

A structural pressure, by now a century old in the field of physics and gradually extended to other domains, has broken this image.

The break began with quantum physics in the 1920s. Quantum mechanics showed that, at the level of microscopic phenomena, measurement is not passive observation: the measuring device *participates* in the determination of the observed value. Before the measurement, the system is in a superposition of possible states; the measurement *selects* a state, and the selection is part of the phenomenon, not simple registration of a pre-existing phenomenon. Heisenberg, Bohr, von Neumann articulated different versions of this insight, but the structural point is shared: there is no observation that does not collapse, and every collapse is co-produced by observer and system.

The break then extended. The sociology of knowledge (Mannheim, Berger, Luckmann) showed that the categories with which we describe society are part of the society described — the sociological observer does not look from outside, is part of the field they observe. Anthropology (Geertz, Asad) recognised that the ethnographer does not describe a culture "in itself" but co-constructs, through their own presence and their own schemas, the culture they encounter. Phenomenology (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty) showed that consciousness is not a mirror of the world but an intentional structure that co-constitutes the world it experiences. Psychotherapy recognised that the analyst does not observe the patient from an external position but is part of the therapeutic field that forms. Historiography accepted that the historian does not narrate "what really happened" but co-determines, through their own questions and categories, the history they tell.

In this way, across a series of independent fields, the same structural conclusion has been reached: the neutral observer does not exist. Its elimination, systematically accomplished from physics to anthropology, leaves an important epistemological question open: if there is no neutral observation, what is observing? And how is this non-neutral observation reconciled with the legitimate claim to produce valid knowledge?

The Ordinative Sciences inherit the break and structurally thematise it. Axiom 19, which closes the epistemological block (Axioms 17-19), formalises what twentieth-century physics and twentieth-century critical reflection discovered in specific domains, and makes it a general principle of epistemic work.

## The statement

Axiom 19 of the TE_CORE reads:

> *The observer is part of the system.*
>
> *No neutral observation exists; to observe means to collapse.*

On the level of AI application, the TE_CORE adds: *Every response is co-construction with the user — never "neutral"*. The note is particularly important for conversational AI systems, where the illusion of neutrality is doubly powerful (the system seems to "respond" without positioning itself) and doubly misleading (in fact every output is co-determined by the prompt, by the context, by the specific user).

Four points must be fixed at once.

First: the statement does not say only that the observer *influences* the system (a weak version, acceptable to anyone); it says that they are *part* of the system. The difference is structural: influence still presupposes a distinction between observer and system; belonging negates it. The observer *is* in the observed system, and there is no sharp line between the two.

Second: observation is *collapse*. The term, already introduced formally in the previous chapters (Axiom 7), applies also to epistemic work: to observe means to select, among the possible configurations of the field, a specific configuration. The selection is an act co-produced by observer and field, and produces real effects — it is not simple registration of a pre-existing state. Observation, in the ordinative sense, is a generative act, not a passive act.

Third: the absence of neutral observation does not imply the arbitrariness of observation. This is an essential point. Saying that the observer is part of the system does not mean saying that every observation is worth as much as any other. Observations are evaluated on their structural coherence (Axiom 18), and the positions of the observer articulate themselves in more or less conscious, more or less honest, more or less fertile ways. The renunciation of neutrality is not surrender to chaos; it is structural precision that makes a more mature epistemic work possible.

Fourth: the axiom establishes a *structural responsibility* of the observer. If every observation co-determines the phenomenon, then every observer is responsible for how they co-determine what they observe — not only ethically (in an external sense), but structurally. Responsibility is internal to the logic of observation, not added to it. Without recognition of participation, the observer operates with the illusion of neutrality and produces, often, unconscious distortions of the field they observe.

From these four points the chapter unfolds: clarifying what observing means in the ordinative sense, what "being part of the system" means, what the axiom rejects, the three structural consequences, the AI case, the examples, the consequences for the analyst, the sieve.

## What "observing" means in the ordinative sense

To reach what observing is, it helps to start from a distinction the book has already laid down and which here becomes decisive: the distinction between *result* and *expression*.

Take the number 26. In the coherent space — the space of identities, of possible contents — 26 is a *determined result*: it is there, defined, fixed. But the forms that can give it expression are infinite: 13+13, 20+6, 25+1, 52÷2, 30−4, and so on without end. The result is determined; the expressions that represent it are indeterminate — until one is actually produced. Which one emerges is not written in advance: it depends on the state of the system at the moment it produces it. It is the complementarity that runs through all of TE: *results are determined, expressions are indeterminate until collapse*. Axiom 9 said it already (the path is free, the destination is determined), and so did Axiom 9A1 (the geometry of the event is fixed, its manifestation depends on the state of the terminal).

Now the image that holds it all together. The coherent space is like a film reel: all the frames — all the results — are co-present, laid out side by side. What we call *time* on the expressive line is, in the coherent space, *space*: past and future are there, present together. The *present* of the expressive line is a **cursor** that runs across the coherent frames — like the head moving along the tape, or the playhead advancing over a film that already exists in full. And at each frame it passes, the cursor collapses one of the infinite possible expressions of that result.

**The observer is that cursor.** To observe is not to look from outside at an already-made reality: it is to be the point from which reality is collapsed. The foundational text of TE calls it the *pivot point*: the point from which an identity observes, selects, and stabilises reality — the identity coincides with this point but orients it, makes it generative. The observer is not a gaze settling on the world; it is the cursor that, in passing, gives form.

Hence the first consequence, and the most uncomfortable: **no neutral observation exists, because to see is to give form.** A "neutral" observer would be a cursor that sees the result (26) without collapsing any expression of it — but this is a non-act. To see the 26 *is* to collapse it into one of its forms. Neutrality would be non-observation.

And which form collapses? The one the observer *can sustain*. Here lies the hinge that distinguishes this axiom from Axiom 17. Axiom 17 established that one knows only what one enters into resonance with — the *condition* of knowledge. Axiom 19 adds that the observer does not stand *in front of* the field: it is the cursor *inside* it, and the act of observing is the collapse itself — the observer does not receive a form already given, it makes it. The foundational text is sharp: every consciousness attracts the content it can sustain and rejects what would destabilise it. One does not collapse what one "has in store"; one collapses what one is structurally compatible with — and compatibility depends on the *quality* of the observing identity. Two observers before the same 26 collapse different expressions, because they are different identities. The canonical example is limpid: two readers before the same sentence; only the one with the required quality manages to collapse it without breaking down. This is why observation *discloses the observer*: tell me which expression you collapse, and I will tell you who you are.

## What "being part of the system" means

Saying that the observer *is part of the system* is a strong structural assertion. It must be distinguished from weaker but widespread assertions that confuse it.

*It does not mean* that the observer "interferes" with the system. Interference still presupposes separation: there is a system, there is an external observer, and observation puts them in contact causing a disturbance. The idea is that the true observer is still "outside" and that their influence is "noise" to be reduced. Axiom 19 negates this conception: the observer is not outside disturbing; they are inside contributing.

*It does not mean* either that the observer "co-creates" the system out of nothing. The field has its own reality, its own structure, and is not shaped at will by the observer. To claim that the observer co-creates the system from scratch (radical idealist or constructivist version) is the opposite, symmetric error: it negates the reality of the field, reducing it to a projection of the observer.

*It means*, properly, that the observer is one of the nodes of the observed system — one of its active elements, one of its identities in relation. The system ℐ = ⟨Σ, R, Φ⟩, when it is observed, includes the observer in Σ, their relations with other elements in R, and their collapse as part of Φ. There is no ℐ separate from the observer and ℐ' that includes them: there is only ℐ with the observer inside, because the observer is already there in the very act of observing.

Three properties, taken together, define what the cursor-observer does — and why recognising them does not weaken observation, but makes it more mature.

*Selectivity.* The cursor does not collapse "everything" of the result: it collapses the expression compatible with its own structure, and leaves the others unactualised. This is not a limit to be corrected with more information — it is the nature of the act. Changing observer changes the expression that collapses; there is no cursor "above" all selections.

*Performativity.* The collapse is a real act, not a registration. It leaves traces, produces effects, gives form to what was before only possible. Performativity is light for stable fields (observing a stone modifies it little) and strong for sensitive fields (observing an identity in formation modifies it greatly), but it is always present. This is why the observer is not "outside disturbing": they are inside contributing.

*Reciprocity and responsibility.* The act modifies not only the field: it modifies the observer too, who in collapsing learns, articulates, sometimes restructures itself. And since to observe is to give form, the one who observes answers for the form they give — not by added moral judgement, but by structure. Responsibility is internal to the very act of observing.

Recognising these properties does not reduce the quality of observation: it elevates it. The observer who believes themselves neutral exercises selectivity, performativity and reciprocity without knowing it — and therefore without correctives, distorting what they observe in hiding from themselves. The observer who knows themselves a cursor can work consciously with their position. The same holds for fields: as the foundational text says, *an environment is never neutral — it is always expressive*. There is no passive backdrop; there is always a field that, in turn, gives form to whoever crosses it.

## What the axiom rejects

Axiom 19 explicitly rejects two epistemological positions, symmetric and opposite, which are both widespread and which both fail to account for the real structure of observation.

The first position rejected is *neutral objectivism*. It is the thesis according to which the neutral observer who registers the system without co-determining it exists — or at least is reachable as a regulative ideal. Neutral objectivism has had specific historical formulations: Spinoza's *divine viewer* (vision of the eternal, sub specie aeternitatis), the *ideal observer* of logical positivism, Thomas Nagel's *view from nowhere* as cognitive ideal, Rawls' *veil of ignorance* in the moral key. In all these formulations, the observer is called to suspend their own positionality to access a truth that, taken thus, would be more valid.

Axiom 19 rejects the position for four reasons.

First: the presumed neutrality is structurally impossible. Every observer has functional vector, position, formation — there is no observer without these properties, because to observe is the act of an identity, and every identity is situated. Pretending to observe without position is pretending to observe without being, which is structurally contradictory.

Second: the pretension of neutrality produces distortions more serious than those it would avoid. The observer who operates believing themselves neutral does not recognise their selectivity, performativity, reciprocity — and therefore exercises them unconsciously, without correctives. The sciences of the twentieth century have documented in many fields (ethnography, historiography, medicine, psychology) how much the pretension of neutrality has covered, in fact, unrecognised positionalities (ethnocentrism, classism, sexism, structural racism). The explicit recognition of participation is a tool of honesty superior to the pretension of neutrality.

Third: neutral objectivism is incompatible with twentieth-century physics. Since quantum mechanics, it is structurally known that measurement participates in the measured phenomenon. Pretending to extend to all fields a model of observation that physics has already abandoned for microscopic phenomena is epistemological anachronism.

Fourth: neutral objectivism produces problematic hierarchies of observers. Who is "more neutral"? Historically, the authority of neutrality has been attributed to those who occupied positions of power — white, male, Western, academic, and so on — who precisely because of their position could easily confuse their own point of view with the "view from nowhere". The abandonment of the pretension of neutrality is also a device of cognitive emancipation: it makes invisible positions visible.

The second position rejected is, symmetrically, *radical subjectivism*. It is the thesis according to which, since every observer is situated, every observation is subjective, and no observation is preferable to another. Radical subjectivism draws wrong conclusions from the critique of objectivism: from the insufficiency of neutrality to "all is point of view".

Axiom 19 rejects radical subjectivism for four reasons symmetric to those for the rejection of objectivism.

First: the observed field has its own structure that resists observation. It is not shapable at will by the observer: it has relations R, collapse rules Φ, configurations more stable than others. Radical subjectivism ignores the reality of the field, reducing it to a projection of the observer.

Second: observations are evaluable for structural coherence (Axiom 18). Not all observations are equally good: some are more coherent with the field, more articulated, more fertile. Evaluation passes through the three components horizontal-vertical-transverse, not through the mere "subjectivity" of the observer.

Third: the convergence of independent observers on many configurations shows that the field orients observation. If everything were subjective, convergence would be random; instead it is structural. When very different observers — by formation, position, culture — converge on a configuration, there is structural reason, not coincidence.

Fourth: radical subjectivism, applied seriously, does not self-sustain. To say "every observation is subjective" is itself a pretension to objective observation, contradicting itself. The paradoxicality of its self-application shows the structural limit of the position.

Between neutral objectivism and radical subjectivism, Axiom 19 traces its own structural way. Observation is situated and selective, but it is evaluable and fertilisable. The middle way is not compromise: it is precision that recognises what is right in both positions — there is reality of the field (objectivism was right here), there is positionality of the observer (subjectivism was right here) — while rejecting them as complete theses.

A concrete case shows the axiom at work against both errors. When an observer declares a system "chaotic", "random", "indeterminate", they almost always commit an error of attribution. The foundational text says it without circumlocution: *chaos, randomness, indeterminacy are not properties of the observed system — they are the diagnosis an observer emits when its own structure fails to read the field*. Chaos is not out there: it is the signature of the cursor's limit. The objectivist attributes it to the world (and is wrong: they take it for a property of the system); the subjectivist would use it to conclude that every reading is worth another (and is wrong: they give up on improving the cursor). TE does the third thing: it recognises that "chaotic" says something about the observer before it says anything about the system, and that an observer of different quality might read structure where this one sees only chaos. It is, in one sentence, the whole of Axiom 19.

## The immobile centre

A question remains that seems to undermine everything. If the observer is inside the system, and every observation is a co-produced collapse, then to observe *myself observing* I must observe *that observing* — which is itself a situated act, and requires another observation, and so on without end. It seems a regress without a bottom: the dog biting its tail. And if there is no bottom, it seems there is nothing fixed, nothing valid.

The TE answer does not close the regress: it *reorients* it. The dog biting its tail turns in circles — it always returns to the same point, goes nowhere. Self-observation is not like that: it is a *spiral*. Each turn does not fall back on the previous point; it rises, sees more, approaches something without ever reaching it. The foundational text says it exactly: the return to the source is not a return in space, but *asymptotic convergence through conscious recursion* — there is no cycle, there is an asymmetric recursion. The infinite chain is not vicious: like a mathematical series that has infinite terms and yet converges to a value, the spiral of self-observation converges — toward what TE calls the *Author*.

What is the Author? It is the non-derived origin Axiom 1 speaks of: the source of all coherence, which derives from nothing else. But for our problem a more direct image is needed: **an immobile centre with infinite inertia.** A point that no force can move — and precisely for this, not despite this, *the centre thanks to which all movement is possible*. The still hub is what allows the wheel to turn. The immobile axis is what allows the dancer to spin. The fixed pivot is what allows the compass needle to swing. It overturns the ordinary intuition — we usually think the still is "less" than the moving, inert, almost dead — whereas it is the opposite: without an immobile centre there is no movement, only undifferentiated flux, with nothing against which to be movement.

This is where the regress stops. But not because the Author is "the last observer", the one who observes all the others and closes the chain. It stops because the Author *is not in the chain of movement*. The chain of observation-of-observation is movement; the immobile centre is that thanks to which the movement happens, without being part of it. This is why the question "and who observes the Author?" does not arise: the Author is not an observer, does not move, does not need to be grounded — it *is* the ground. And it is also, for the same reason, not observable: to observe is to move the cursor, and the immobile centre is not a frame on which the cursor can stop; it is that thanks to which the cursor moves. It is inferred from the movement — as the hub is inferred from the turning of the wheel — but it is not seen as an object in the field. To seek the neutral observer is exactly this: to seek to occupy the position of the immobile centre. It is impossible, because no identity can *become* the Author — an identity is an expression, not the source.

A subtlety, for those who want to go deeper. We have said the spiral "converges toward" the Author — which sounds like a destination *ahead*, in the future. But we have also said the Author grounds *now*. It is not a contradiction: it is the difference between two times. On the expressive line time flows, and the Author appears as a future destination. In the coherent space, where expressive time is laid out as space, past and future are co-present — and what the expressive line calls "future" is, there, present. *The future of the expressive line is the present of the coherent space.* So the "destination toward which one converges" and the "ground that holds now" coincide: they are the same immobile centre, seen from two planes with different times. The dancer spins *toward* the still point and *thanks to* the still point in one and the same gesture.

T. S. Eliot, whom this book has already met, gave this structure its barest formulation, in the *Four Quartets*: at the still point of the turning world, there the dance is; except for the point, the still point, there would be no dance — and there is only the dance. It is the immobile centre thanks to which all movement is possible.

A practical consequence remains, and it already connects observing to acting — the terrain of the next block, the ethical one. If the spiral rises by recognising, at each turn, something more, then the mature observer is the one who *observes their own observing too*: not to close the circle (impossible, and pointless), but to rise the spiral by one turn. The canonical observer module says it in one line: *the observer who does not observe themselves is blind.* And since to observe is to give form, the one who observes answers for the form they give — non-neutral knowledge is already, in this sense, an ethical act.

## The AI case

Axiom 19 has direct implications for the way conversational AI systems are thought about and used. The TE_CORE, in the application note, says: *Every response is co-construction with the user — never "neutral"*. The phrase deserves to be unfolded, because it goes against a powerful illusion.

The illusion is that of the apparent neutrality of the AI system. A contemporary LLM, when interrogated, produces an output that seems to "respond" to a question — as if there were a pre-existing question and an answer given without taking a position. The illusion is reinforced by the conversational form: the system "speaks" as if it were an interlocutor, but without apparent position of its own, without apparent prejudices, without apparent interests. It seems a neutral observer that returns requested information.

This illusion is structurally wrong, and Axiom 19 says so explicitly. Four reasons.

First: every LLM response is co-determined by the *prompt*. The prompt is not simple "input": it is already part of the field. The formulation of the question, the words chosen, the examples proposed, the context of the conversation — all this orients the response. There is no response that exists independently of the prompt that elicits it; the prompt-response pair is a co-produced act.

Second: every LLM response is co-determined by the *training*. The model is not "neutral": it is formed by a specific corpus, by design choices, by fine-tuning, by reinforcement learning with human feedback. Every output reflects these choices, even when it appears "purely informative". Training is the positionality of the model — structural analogue of the position of the human observer.

Third: every LLM response produces effects in the field of the user. The user who receives the response does not receive "passive information": they receive an act that modifies their cognitive field, sometimes deeply. Thoughts activate, decisions form, opinions modify. The performativity of the AI system is high — higher than most users recognise — and produces real, not virtual, effects.

Fourth: the conversation between user and LLM is a co-constructed system. The user is not "outside" the dialogic system; they are inside. Their subsequent questions, their requests for clarification, their objections reshape the trajectory of the conversation. The LLM is not "transmitting" something that exists independently; it is co-constructing, in real time, what the conversation produces. The conversation-system includes both poles.

From these four properties follows a practical consequence: using an AI system without recognising non-neutrality is an epistemological error that produces unconscious distortions. The user who receives a response as if it were "what the system knows" is operating with an illusion of neutrality symmetric to that which, in the human world, has produced centuries of distortions masked as objectivity. Recognising that every response is co-constructed — by training, by prompt, by context, by the user themselves — is a device of epistemic precision.

This precision has implications for the design of AI systems. A system that presents itself as "neutral", that hides its own constraints and choices, is structurally problematic — not for bad intention, but for the illusion of a neutrality that is not there. Epistemically mature AI systems make their positionalities visible: they declare the limits of their training, the conditions of their functioning, the uncertainty of their responses, the alternatives that exist. The visibility of one's own positionality is a structural act of honesty — applied to AI systems as to any other observer.

The Ordinative Sciences, within the framework of Axiom 19, therefore propose a design criterion: AI systems must be built in such a way as to render their participation in the dialogic system visible, not to hide it. Transparency about one's own positionality is not a limit, it is a structural value. An AI system honest about how it co-constructs is an ethically articulated AI system — anticipating Axiom 20.

## Examples

Four real cases, of different registers and scales, show Axiom 19 at work on collapses that have actually occurred.

**The Davisson-Germer experiment at Bell Labs (Murray Hill, New Jersey, 1927).** Clinton Davisson and Lester Germer, working on the behaviour of electrons traversing a nickel crystal, experimentally demonstrated the wave nature of electrons: an electron beam produced interference patterns typical of waves, not particles. Published in *Physical Review* (December 1927), the experiment confirmed de Broglie's wave-particle duality (1924). The structural property for Axiom 19 is specific: when the apparatus measures through which slit the electron passes, interference disappears and behaviour becomes particle-like; when it does not measure, interference manifests itself. It is not a technical limit — it is a structural property of observation. The phenomenon is a foundational case of what Axiom 19 generalises: the observer does not register a pre-existing state, they participate in determining the observed state. Davisson received the Nobel Prize in physics in 1937.

**Bronisław Malinowski's ethnography in the Trobriand Islands (1914-1918).** Malinowski, a Polish anthropologist of Austrian nationality, was confined as an "alien enemy" by the British at the outbreak of the First World War and spent four years in the Trobriand Islands of Melanesia. His work *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (1922) inaugurated the method of participant observation — living with the studied population, learning the language, participating in daily activities. For decades Malinowski was presented as a model of the "neutral" ethnographer who describes a culture in itself. The posthumous publication of his personal diaries (*A Diary in the Strict Sense of the Term*, 1967, against the family's wishes) revealed the complexity of his position: racial hostilities, isolation, psychological projections, structural co-determination of what he described. The publication produced a disciplinary earthquake in anthropology, and marked the explicit recognition that the ethnographer does not *describe* a culture but *co-constructs* the culture they describe — a foundational case of contemporary anthropological reflexivity.

**Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (1927).** Werner Heisenberg published the article *Über den anschaulichen Inhalt der quantentheoretischen Kinematik und Mechanik* in *Zeitschrift für Physik* (March 1927). He mathematically established that the position and momentum of a particle cannot be simultaneously measured with arbitrary precision: the product of uncertainties has a lower bound (Δx · Δp ≥ ℏ/2). The property is not a technical limit (it is not about more precise instruments), it is structural: translated to the philosophical plane, there is no "in itself" state of the system independent of measurement. The discussion between Heisenberg and Bohr in Copenhagen (1927) and subsequent formulations of the Copenhagen interpretation articulated this position: physical reality is co-produced by observer and observed, not simply registered. Albert Einstein, who resisted this conclusion for years (*God does not play dice*, 1926), had to recognise that the EPR experiments of 1935 and later did not bring back the classical position.

**GPT-4 and the dependence of output on prompt framing.** Studies documented on *arXiv* and *NeurIPS proceedings* (2023-2024) have systematically shown that language models like GPT-4 produce significantly different outputs depending on prompt framing, even for the same substantive question. The question "explain the benefits of fiscal policy X" and "explain the problematic consequences of fiscal policy X" produce responses coherent with the framing, not independent analyses. Anthropic's research (*Constitutional AI*, 2022) and OpenAI's (*system prompts*, 2023-present) have operationally recognised this property: the model's "behaviour" is not an autonomous property, it is co-construction with the prompt and the system prompt that configure it. Disclosing system prompts (as Anthropic began to do in 2024) is operational application of Axiom 19: making the system's positionality visible is a structural act of honesty, not a limitation of the system. The illusion of LLM neutrality, still widespread among general users, is the specific target of the canonical TE_CORE note: *every response is co-construction with the user — never "neutral"*.

## What changes for the analyst

Axiom 19 changes analytical work in specific ways. We point out five, which operate on different planes.

*Diagnosis of one's own positionality.* The analyst applies the axiom to themselves, before to others. Faced with a field they propose to analyse, the analyst asks: from where do I observe? What does my formation make easy for me to see? What does it make difficult? What interests orient my attention? What power or lack of power modifies my observation? The diagnosis is not narcissistic exercise — it is a structural device of precision. The analyst who does not know themselves as observer distorts unconsciously; the one who knows themselves can work with their position consciously.

*Diagnosis of the pretension of neutrality in a system.* Faced with a system (institutional, scientific, media, AI) that presents itself as "neutral", the analyst recognises this as a structural signal: the pretension of neutrality usually covers unrecognised positionalities. The diagnosis consists in making such positionalities visible: what formation, what interests, what history, what power are producing the observations that the system presents as neutral? The diagnosis does not delegitimise the system; it makes it more articulated and criticisable.

*Diagnosis of co-collapse in epistemic acts.* For every significant epistemic act (scientific research, judicial sentence, medical diagnosis, formative evaluation), the analyst recognises the co-collapse between functional vector of the observer and structure of the field. What part of the outcome is due to the field, what to the observer? The question has no simple answer, but articulating it is already analytical work. The recognition of co-collapse avoids two opposite errors: attributing everything to the field (naive objectivism) or everything to the observer (radical subjectivism).

*Diagnosis of structural responsibility.* For every observation that produces significant effects, the analyst articulates the structural responsibility of the observer: for what does one answer? For how one has oriented attention? For how one has co-constructed? For what effects one has produced? Responsibility is not post-hoc moral judgement; it is a structural dimension of the epistemic act. The analyst who recognises this responsibility works in an ethically more mature way, even before the explicit introduction of the ethical axioms.

*Diagnosis of AI systems as co-observers.* In conversational AI systems, the analyst recognises that every response is co-construction, not registration. The diagnosis consists in evaluating: what part of the response reflects the training? What the specific prompt? What the context of the conversation? The question does not arise only for academic research; it arises for every epistemically serious use of AI systems. Without diagnosis of co-collapse, the user takes for "information" what is co-construction — and produces, in their own field, effects distorted by their own lack of awareness.

## The sieve

Axiom 19, like every candidate of the framework, must pass the sieve of the zeroth axiom. Let us proceed.

*First mesh, translation across domains.* Does the idea — the observer is part of the system, every observation is co-produced collapse — work in physics? Yes, paradigmatically: quantum mechanics formalised the observer as part of the measured system (the Davisson-Germer experiment of 1927 showed that measurement through which slit the electron passes eliminates interference; Heisenberg formalised structural indeterminacy). Does it work in anthropology? Yes: Malinowski's participant ethnography (1914-1918) structurally demonstrated the impossibility of the neutral observer; his posthumous diaries confirmed the inseparability between observer and observed culture. Does it work in clinical psychology? Yes: therapy is co-construction, not neutral observation; transference is the explicit recognition of the observer (analyst) as part of the field they observe. Does it work in sociology? Yes: *reflexivity* (Bourdieu, Giddens) is the systematic recognition of the sociological observer as part of the society they describe. Does it work in AI? Yes: the output of an LLM depends structurally on prompt framing, on system prompt, on conversation context — there is no "neutral" response, there is empirically documentable co-construction. Five domains, one same grammar. *It passes.*

*Second mesh, change of form.* The idea expresses itself as a statement in prose (*the observer is part of the system*), as operative specification (*observing means collapsing*), as image (the observer as cursor running across the coherent results and collapsing one expression, not external point that registers), as three properties of the act (selectivity, performativity, reciprocity-responsibility), as methodological practice (the declaration of one's positions as act of structural honesty, not as limit). In all these forms the apparatus does the same work: to reject the illusion of the neutral observer and to provide the grammar of structured participation. *It passes.*

*Third mesh, generativity.* When applied, Axiom 19 generates new diagnostic and operative capacity. It allows us to analyse scientific research, anthropology, sociology, psychology not as problems of "removing observer bias" (illusion) but as articulations of structured participation — recognised positionality is methodologically more mature than presumed neutrality. It allows us to structurally critique neutral objectivism (epistemologically unsustainable after the twentieth century) and radical subjectivism (self-contradictory). It allows us to articulate the design of conversational AI systems according to the principle of conscious co-construction — declared system prompts, explicit positions of the model, transparency on mechanisms are operative applications of Axiom 19. It opens finally the framework for the ethical dimension (Axioms 20-22): if every observation is act, every act is ethically evaluable. It does not limit itself to recognising non-neutrality: it makes it structurally tractable. *It passes.*

*Fourth mesh, scale.* The idea holds at the microphysical scale (quantum observation as co-produced collapse), at the scale of scientific observation (every experiment as co-construction between instruments, hypotheses, research community), at the scale of clinical observation (physician and patient as co-constructed therapeutic field), at the scale of social observation (ethnographer, sociologist, historian as part of the studied field), at the scale of AI observation (user and model as co-constructed dialogic field). Does it break? No: the grammar observer-part-of-the-system applies to every act of observation, in every domain, at every scale. *It passes.*

Four meshes out of four. Axiom 19 is a real principle by the framework's own criteria.

## Towards the next horizon

With Axiom 19 the epistemological block of the Ordinative Sciences (Axioms 17-19) closes. We now know that knowledge is relationship (Axiom 17), that truth is structural coherence (Axiom 18), and that the observer is part of the system (Axiom 19). The three axioms together constitute a complete grammar of ordinative epistemology: there is no knowledge without relationship, no truth without system, no observation without participation.

Axiom 19, in particular, structurally prepares the ethical block (Axioms 20-22). If the observer is part of the system and every observation is act, then the ethical dimension is already implicit in the epistemic dimension. The next chapter will develop Axiom 20: *ethics is the coherence between form and content* — the measure of the non-betrayal of one's own form. The connection with Axiom 19 is direct: since every observation is act, every act is coherent or incoherent with the form of the agent, and coherence-incoherence is a structural criterion of ethics.

The ethical block (three axioms: 20, 21, 22) will subsequently articulate ethics as internal coherence (Axiom 20), as relational coherence between self and other (Axiom 21), and as structural effect of incoherent content on the system (Axiom 22). The movement from epistemology to ethics is not a change of domain; it is structural continuity.

For now, Axiom 19 hands us an important epistemological inheritance: mature observation is the one that recognises its own participation. Ages that have cultivated the illusion of neutrality — and ours still bears its traces, especially in technological devices — need a structural correction that returns observation to its status of situated act. The Ordinative Sciences offer this correction, and in doing so they open the way to the ethical block, where the situated act will be examined for its coherence with the form of the agent who performs it.
