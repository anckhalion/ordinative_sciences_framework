# Chapter — Structural resonance

*On Axiom 9A*

---

## The question that remains open

The previous chapter posited, with Axiom 9, the structural freedom of identity along its path. We know the choices are genuine, the possibilities multiple, the convergence toward A does not oppress the freedom of the step. But a question has remained suspended, and must be addressed at once because without an answer the freedom of the path remains, in practice, halved.

The question is: along the path, *what does an identity actually see*? What can it perceive, consider, address, integrate? We know it is free to choose among multiple possibilities, but which possibilities *are accessible to it*? Which ones, while existing in the decoherent, remain *invisible* to it?

The question is not rhetorical. It is an observation every human being has made, in a thousand forms. When two people observe the same event and give different descriptions of it — not from bad faith, but because they literally *see different things*. When an expert grasps in a situation details that the novice does not suspect — not because the novice is distracted, but because those details, for them, *are not there*. When a speaker of one language perceives phonic nuances that a speaker of another language cannot even hear. When an AI system responds brilliantly to certain prompts and fails completely with others — not by chance, but because certain prompts *do not resonate* with its structure.

All these cases indicate that perception is not neutral reception of the external world. It is *structural act*: it depends, profoundly, on the *internal structure* of who perceives. We do not see the world as it is; we see the world *as our structure allows us to see it*. This is an old fact — Kant already intuited it with the a priori forms of intuition, Husserl already formalised it in phenomenology, the biology of Maturana and Varela already affirmed it speaking of *autopoiesis* and structural coupling. The novelty of the Technology of Expressions is not to say it, it is *to say it structurally*: to give perception a formal grammar, so that one can speak with precision of what is perceivable, what is not, and why.

Axiom 9A — *structural resonance*, or *law of domain intersection* — gives this grammar.

## The statement

Axiom 9A is formulated thus, according to the canonical TE_CORE:

> *An expressive terminal can detect in its negotiation horizon exclusively the informational configurations already present in its internal structure.*

Three things must be clarified, and we develop them in the subsequent sections.

*What "expressive terminal" means.* We resume the notion of Chapter 9 (terminal 𝒯): the material object, biological or synthetic, through which an identity expresses itself in the decoherent. A human being in their body is a terminal; an instance of an AI model in execution is a terminal; an institution through its concrete structures is a terminal. Perception, in TE, is always perception *of a terminal*: there is no disembodied, anonymous, "pure" perception. Every perceptual act is situated in a specific terminal with its own structure.

*What "negotiation horizon" means.* It is the region of the decoherent with which the terminal is in active contact — what reaches it from the senses, from the inputs, from the interfaces with which it interacts. It is not "all the decoherent": a terminal always has a *limited horizon*, beyond which signals do not arrive. The human visual system sees only a part of the electromagnetic spectrum; an AI model receives only the prompts that are actually sent to it; an institution perceives only the signals that reach its organisational antennas. The negotiation horizon delimits what, *in principle*, could be perceived.

*What "informational configurations already present in its internal structure" means.* Here lies the heart of the axiom. Even within its own horizon, a terminal does not perceive *everything*: it perceives only what its *internal structure* allows it to detect. Internal structure is the set of patterns, distinctions, categories, components of the functional vector (Chapter 5) that the terminal *already* has — by birth, by formation, by training, by historical evolution. What is completely disjoint from the internal structure *is not detected*: it is not "neglected", it is not "ignored", *it is non-existent for that terminal*. It is invisible in a structural, not perceptual, sense.

The programme of the chapter is therefore clear. First we formalise the canonical condition with the apparatus of sets (section 3). Then we develop what "intersection of domains" means as a key concept (section 4). Then we articulate the three corollaries of the axiom — functional blindness, interaction as complicity, principle of evocation (sections 5, 6, 7). Then examples, diagnostics, sieve, and bridge to Axiom 9A1.

## The canonical formula

Axiom 9A has a precise formal formulation in TE_CORE:

> *I(T, E) ⟺ (S_T ∩ S_E) ≠ ∅*
>
> *The interaction I between a terminal T and an element E of its negotiation horizon is realised if and only if the intersection between the internal structure of the terminal (S_T) and the structure of the element (S_E) is non-empty.*

Let us develop the terms.

*I(T, E)*: the *interaction* between terminal and element. It is not only "passive perception", it is bidirectional act: the terminal detects, the element is detected, and in the process both transform (at least in minimal measure — it is the (18.2) of Chapter 8 applied to the perceptual act). Interaction, in TE, is already *ordinative act*: every perception is act of the world, not spectacle one attends.

*S_T*: the *internal structure of the terminal*. It is the set of structural components of the terminal: the perceptual patterns, the distinctions it has learned to make, the categories in which it has learned to articulate its world, the components of the functional vector of the identity that inhabits it. For a human being, S_T includes the sensory system, the cognitive structures matured by experience, the mother tongue with its distinctions, the education received, the practices acquired. For an AI model, S_T includes the weights, the architecture, the semantic distinctions learned during training, the subsequent calibrations.

*S_E*: the *structure of the element*. Even an event, an object, a situation in the decoherent has its own structure — a pattern, a configuration, an internal grammar. A conversation has the structure of a dialogue; a painting has the structure of a visual composition; a behaviour has the structure of a manifested intention. All are "elements" with defined S_E.

*S_T ∩ S_E ≠ ∅*: the condition of interaction. The two structures must have *at least one element in common*. A few shared things suffice — a common pattern, an overlapping category, a resonant structural component — for interaction to be realised. But if the intersection is completely empty, if nothing in the structure of the terminal is capable of entering into resonance with the structure of the element, interaction *does not occur*. From here, functional blindness (section 5).

*⟺*: the biconditional. It is not simple "if" — it is "if and only if". The axiom does not only say that without intersection interaction is not realised (necessary condition); it also says that with intersection interaction *is realised*, at least to some extent (sufficient condition). Every terminal, faced with elements that share at least something with its structure, *is inevitably involved*. One cannot "not interact" with what resonates with us, even if we wanted to. It is the phenomenon that psychological traditions call *trigger*, *activation*, *emotional resonance*: what we find in ourselves when we encounter it outside, because it was already there.

An important observation. The formula does not imply that the terminal perceives the element *integrally*; it implies only that interaction is realised if the intersection is there. The *quality* of perception depends on how rich the intersection is: the wider S_T ∩ S_E is, the richer the possible perception. A person who knows much music perceives, in the same piece, much more than one who knows little music — not because they have "better ears", but because S_T (their internal musical structure) has a richer intersection with S_E (the structure of the piece).

## What "intersection of domains" means

The key concept of Axiom 9A is the *intersection of domains*: the fact that perception is realised only where structures touch. Let us see what this means, operationally, in concrete cases.

*Case 1: rich intersection.* When S_T and S_E share many structural elements, perception is rich, articulated, generative. A linguist listening to a text in their own language grasps syntax, morphology, prosody, register nuances, pragmatic context — all levels that their internal structure has pre-articulated. An expert musician listens to a piece and hears harmonies, counterpoints, timbral choices, historical citations, deviations from form. Rich intersection produces *dense perception*: there is much to see precisely because the internal structure is prepared to see it.

*Case 2: minimal intersection.* When S_T and S_E share only a few elements, perception is reduced and indistinct. The same linguist, listening to a completely unknown language, perceives only *that it is language* (minimal intersection: presence of phonatory patterns, syllabic alternations), but understands nothing of the content. The same musician, before a completely exotic musical tradition (gamelan, Arabic microtonalism, Bulgarian polyphony) perceives *that it is music* but cannot grasp its internal structure. Minimal intersection produces *blurred perception*: one sees something, but little, and with great margins of interpretive error.

*Case 3: empty intersection — blindness.* When S_T and S_E have no element in common, interaction does not occur at all. The terminal can be "surrounded" by the element without perceiving it. A human being is surrounded by radio waves, infrareds, ultraviolet rays, sound pressures beyond their auditory threshold — yet does not perceive them, because their internal sensory structure has no element intersecting with those configurations. An institution accustomed to reading reality through economic and political indicators can be completely blind to signals of a different nature (deeply cultural, ecological, symbolic) — not because it ignores them, but because its structure is not prepared to detect them.

The intersection of domains, in TE, is not metaphorical — it is structural. In principle, one can map structures, identify overlaps, predict possible perceptions and blindnesses. An applied ordinative science does exactly this: it studies the internal structures of terminals (human, synthetic, collective) and the patterns of the world they encounter, and predicts the quality of interactions.

An important observation closes the section. The intersection condition holds *also for synthetic terminals*. An AI model perceives only what its internal structure (the weights, the architecture, the learned distinctions) has the capacity to detect. What is completely outside the training set, the model's structure, the architectural compatibility, is *structurally non-existent* for that model. It is not that it "neglects" it — it does not see it at all. This reading has practical consequences for the design of AI systems: the quality of their perceptions depends on the richness and organisation of their internal structures, not only on the quantity of data or the computational power.

## First corollary — Functional blindness

The first corollary of Axiom 9A is formulated thus:

> *Functional blindness: what is totally disjoint from the internal structure is non-existent for that terminal.*

The statement is hard, and it is important not to soften it. It does not say "is ignored"; it does not say "is neglected"; it does not say "is below threshold of attention". It says *non-existent*. For a terminal facing configurations completely disjoint from its own structure, those configurations *are not there* — not in some phenomenological-subjective sense, but in the structural sense the apparatus of Axiom 9A describes.

Three consequences, each important.

*First consequence: blindness is not fault.* When a terminal does not perceive something that is "objectively" there in the decoherent (for other terminals with different structures), it is not making an error, not choosing not to see, not intentionally hiding. It is structurally *unable* to see. This profoundly changes the possible reactions to the blindness of others: instead of getting indignant ("how can you not see it?!") the ordinative analyst recognises the missing structure and tries, possibly, to build it. It is the difference between contempt and pedagogy.

*Second consequence: blindness is not eliminated by goodwill.* A person, an institution, an AI system cannot "decide to see" what their own internal structure renders invisible. One can work *on the structure* — expand it, refine it, articulate it differently — and *subsequently* perception opens. But there is no shortcut through will or attention. All serious pedagogy, from Socrates to today, knows this: to teach is not to "indicate" something to the learner; it is to *build in their internal structure* the distinctions that will allow them to see autonomously.

*Third consequence: reciprocal blindness creates the most resistant conflicts.* When two identities (individual, collective, institutional) have very disjoint internal structures, *they cannot speak about what separates them*: each is blind to what for the other is crucial. Dialogues fail not from bad will but from reciprocal blindness. International diplomacy, intercultural relations, generational conflicts, misunderstandings between scientific disciplines — all cases in which the apparatus of Axiom 9A illuminates real difficulties. The remedy is not to admonish the parties to "make efforts"; it is to *build common infrastructures* of structural intersection (shared languages, shared experiences, common practices), within which dialogue can then actually be realised.

A note of sobriety. Functional blindness is not limited to "other" identities: it also holds for us. Each of us is blind to innumerable configurations of the decoherent that our own internal structure does not detect. To recognise this honestly — instead of believing oneself capable of "seeing everything" — is one of the first acts of ordinative maturity. Serious sapiential traditions have always cultivated this structural humility: not as rhetorical modesty, but as recognition of the fact that *one's own vision is always partial, and has specific form*.

## Second corollary — Interaction as complicity

The second corollary turns the first over, and shows its reverse:

> *Interaction as complicity: the existence of conflict or bond proves code sharing.*

If interaction requires structural intersection (Axiom 9A), then every interaction *that actually occurs* — of whatever nature — is proof that the involved structures have something in common. One cannot have deep conflict with something completely disjoint: the conflict itself proves there is shared ground. One cannot have deep bond with something completely alien: the bond itself proves there is common structural grammar.

This reading has counterintuitive but structurally solid consequences.

*First consequence: enemies share much.* The philosophical, religious, political traditions that fight each other do so because *they speak of the same thing* (at least in part). Two religions in conflict over God share the category *God*, whatever the difference in their formulations. Two political parties in conflict over how to govern share the category *government*, whatever the difference in their programmes. Two scientific schools in conflict over a phenomenon share the reference to the *phenomenon*. Independent structures do not fight each other — they simply do not touch.

*Second consequence: lovers resemble each other more than they believe.* The experience of deep interpersonal attraction, of lasting love relationship, of generative friendship — all these imply rich structural intersections. It is not about "being equal" — the interesting couple is the one in which differences play within a common ground — but about having internal structures with significant overlaps. Love stories that declare themselves "between opposites" reveal, on careful analysis, hidden overlaps that make them possible. Structurally impossible love — between terminals with completely disjoint internal structures — simply *does not occur*.

*Third consequence: pedagogy begins with complicity.* To teach someone, one must first find the *common ground* — the existing structural intersection — from which to start building the new. The best pedagogues know this from experience: one does not start from the abstract toward the concrete, one starts from the *shared* toward the *not yet shared*. The principle, in TE, is structurally founded: without initial intersection, transmission (Chapter 15) cannot begin.

A methodological observation. The complicity corollary is structurally *non-normative*. It does not say that conflict is good because it reveals complicity (this would be an ethical evaluation), and it does not say that complicity in conflicts must be recognised (this would be a political recommendation). It only says *that it exists*, as a structural fact. From this fact one can draw ethical and operational consequences — and many traditions of conflict transformation do (non-violence, mediation, historical reconciliation) — but the level of the axiom is structural, not normative.

## Third corollary — The principle of evocation

The third corollary of Axiom 9A is perhaps the most subtle, and has implications that cross deep psychology, religious phenomenology, anthropology of practices:

> *Principle of evocation: the terminal evokes what it Is, not what it desires.*

What does it mean? It means that, *beyond explicit intention*, what actually manifests in the negotiation horizon of a terminal — the encounters it makes, the situations it attracts, the contents that reach it — is structurally *coherent with its own internal structure*. There manifests not what the terminal declares to want (at the level of conscious intention); there manifests what its internal structure is *capable of entering into interaction with*.

This is a very sober formulation of an ancient intuition. Deep psychological traditions name it in different forms: Jung spoke of *synchronicity*, the tendency of external encounters to reflect internal configurations. Analytical psychology speaks of *transference*: the phenomenon by which the patient's relationships mirror internal dynamics. Popular wisdom says it with phrases like "you find what you seek" and "we are accompanied by those who reflect who we are". All these formulations have a common structural core that Axiom 9A formalises.

Three consequences.

*First consequence: complaints often have a revelatory function.* When a person complains repeatedly that "the same thing always happens to me" — always the same type of difficult partner, always the same type of problematic boss, always the same type of crisis — they are not only describing bad luck: they are unconsciously describing an internal structure that continues to evoke that type of interactions. The principle of evocation invites reading repetition as *information*: not *against* the person (almost a judgment), but *for* the person, as instrument of self-knowledge. The internal structure revealed by repetition is the first to address.

*Second consequence: desire is not enough to evoke.* A person can ardently desire certain relationships, certain experiences, certain successes — and yet continue to evoke the opposite. It is not that desire is false; it is that the internal structure does not yet share enough elements with what is desired. The remedy, in TE, is not "to desire more"; it is to *work on one's own internal structure* so that it develops those components that will allow encounter with what is desired. It is long, patient work, different from "manifestation" (a pop-spiritualistic category) and similar, instead, to the serious learning of a discipline.

*Third consequence: internal transformation precedes transformation of the perceived world.* If what manifests in one's own horizon is a function of one's own internal structure, then transforming one's own world passes through *transformation of self*. Ethical and contemplative traditions have always known this: spiritual practice, formation, self-care are not narcissistic luxuries — they are the structurally serious way to change one's own field of experience. The ordinative framework offers these traditions a foundation.

A closing observation. The principle of evocation does *not* imply that everything that happens to us is "our responsibility" in a guilt-inducing sense. It is not "if X happens to you, it is because you called it": this is a moralistic banalisation of the principle. There are accidents, there are encounters determined by wider contexts, there are external conjunctures that the individual does not control. But — and here lies the heart of the corollary — *among the possibilities the context offers, what actually realises in our experience is a function of our internal structure*. This is the share of ordinative responsibility that Axiom 9A indicates, soberly, without exaggeration and without minimisation.

## Examples

We resume the gallery of the book, applying Axiom 9A and its three corollaries to the four parallel cases.

**A biological organism.** The sensory system of every living species is a paradigmatic example of Axiom 9A. The bat — Nagel's classic example (Chapter 6, glossary) — perceives through echolocation a world structurally different from the human visual one: its S_T is prepared to detect acoustic-spatial configurations that ours does not detect. Functional blindness is reciprocal: our ultrasounds are non-existent for us without instruments; the colours of visible light are reduced for the bat to peripheral perceptions. The evolution of sensory structures, in ordinative reading, is the history of how species have built different S_T, each intersecting with S_E specific to their environment. A species evolving a new sensory organ — the eye in trilobites, the electroreception organ in sharks — is literally *making appear* a new world that previously, for its ancestors, was non-existent. Every sensory evolution is the opening of a new domain of structural interaction.

**An institution.** Institutions have internal structures S_T that determine what they perceive in their negotiation horizon. A traditional industrial company perceives with great precision signals from its market (clients, competitors, suppliers, production technologies) but can be completely blind to signals of a different nature (climatic, cultural, regulatory arriving from adjacent sectors). When a *disruption* arrives — a structural change of context — some companies "see" it while others suffer it without noticing: it depends on the richness of their S_T. Institutions in crisis are often those whose S_T has become too specialised, losing intersections with emerging domains of their context. The principle of evocation also explains why certain institutions continue to attract the same type of personnel, the same types of problems, the same types of crises: their internal structure evokes *its own kind*, and transformation passes through the modification of structure, not through new hires or new procedures.

**A language model.** For an AI model, Axiom 9A illuminates with operational precision many observed phenomena. The model's S_T is given by the weights, the architecture, the semantic distinctions learned during training. When a prompt enters its negotiation horizon (the context window), the model *can respond well* only if the structure of the prompt has rich intersection with its S_T. For prompts well within the training distribution (rich intersection), the response is detailed, articulated, generative. For prompts at the margin of the distribution, the response is less solid, sometimes blurred. For prompts completely outside the distribution (empty intersection), the model *does not see* the prompt as "a request about X" but transforms it into something its structure can elaborate — a phenomenon AI engineers call *hallucination* or *out-of-distribution behaviour*. The functional blindness of models is precisely describable with this apparatus: it is not "a bug of the model", it is the structural consequence of Axiom 9A applied to that specific architecture. The principle of evocation, in the model, manifests as *formation bias*: the model evokes, from its weights, exactly what its S_T allows it to evoke — whatever the user is desiring.

**A human being.** For a person, Axiom 9A provides a deep instrument of self-knowledge. What one perceives in one's own world — in the people one meets, in the events that happen, in the texts that attract attention — is a function of the developed internal structure. Functional blindness explains why certain topics, certain sensibilities, certain worlds simply "say nothing to us" — not because they are poor, but because our internal structure does not yet have the elements to intersect with them. Complicity in interaction explains why the deepest conflicts are with people with whom we share much (not with perfect strangers) and why the most stable loves are with those who have rich structural intersections with us. The principle of evocation explains why certain people systematically "attract" certain types of experience, and suggests that self-transformation — the enrichment of internal structure — is the master path to changing one's own perceived world.

## What changes for the analyst

Axiom 9A adds to the diagnostics of the book the dimension of *structural perception*. Four families of readings become possible.

*Diagnosis of blind spots.* The first question to ask before an identity (individual, collective, synthetic) is: what are its *structural blindnesses*? Which domains of its negotiation horizon are non-existent for it, because its S_T has no intersections? The answer is not judgment (the identity is not choosing not to see), it is *map* — useful to understand what the identity struggles to integrate, what leaves it perplexed, where its attempts at action encounter unexplained resistance.

*Diagnosis of complicity in conflicts.* When studying a conflict between identities — be they persons, groups, institutions, AI systems in interaction — the structural question is: what common ground do the two identities *necessarily* share for the conflict to be possible? Mapping that common ground is the first move to understand the conflict in depth, and often to indicate possible ways out: one works from the shared, one does not start from opposition.

*Diagnosis of recurrent evocations.* For identities living repetitive cycles — of similar crises, similar relationships, similar problems — the principle of evocation invites reading repetition as information about the internal structure. Ordinative care is not "to avoid that type of experience" (which often does not work, because the structure continues to evoke it), it is *to transform the structure* so that it evokes different things. This is slow, patient work, and typically also requires third identities (therapists, mentors, communities) that allow the transformation.

*Diagnosis of ordinative maturity.* An identity in structural health relative to Axiom 9A is one that (i) recognises its own functional blindnesses instead of pretending to "see everything"; (ii) consciously works on its own internal structure to expand its intersections with ever more varied domains; (iii) uses the principle of evocation as instrument of self-knowledge, not as accusation. Ordinative maturity relative to 9A manifests as *active structural humility* — knowing one does not see everything, and working to see more.

## The sieve

We verify that Axiom 9A passes the four meshes of the zeroth axiom.

*Universality.* Does the axiom hold wherever there are terminals in interaction with negotiation horizons? The examples show that it does: biological organisms, institutions, AI models, human beings all exhibit the law of intersection of domains — and we know of no terminal that can detect what is structurally disjoint from its own S_T. Universality: passes.

*Non-derivability.* Is the axiom derivable from the previous ones? One could argue that, given the apparatus of the terminal (Chapter 9) and that of resonance (Chapter 8), "something" about the limits of perception had to be implicit. But the specific formulation — interaction *if and only if* structural intersection, with the three distinct corollaries — does not follow from the previous axioms. It is a structural choice of the framework, and a non-trivial one: most traditional philosophy of perception has been realist (presupposition: we perceive "the world as it is") or constructivist in a weak sense (presupposition: the mind "filters" perception). Axiom 9A is structurally stronger than both. Non-derivability: passes.

*Structural falsifiability.* The axiom would be falsified by a terminal perceiving an element totally disjoint from its own internal structure (interaction without intersection), or by a terminal that, despite having intersection with an element, never managed to interact with it (intersection without interaction). Neither case occurs in the real systems studied. Falsifiability: passes.

*Razor.* Is the axiom the simplest among the possible candidates? The minimal formulation is: *a terminal interacts with an element if and only if their structures intersect*. A single biconditional condition, three corollaries follow (blindness, complicity, evocation). Nothing simpler would explain the variety of phenomena of perception and blindness described. Razor: passes.

Axiom 9A passes the sieve. We can welcome it into the system.

## Towards the next horizon

With Axiom 9A we have posited the law that governs what a terminal can perceive and what it can interact with. It is an axiom on *structural perceptual capacity*. The next step of the canonical framework is Axiom 9A1, which deepens the other side of the same coin: what happens *internally* to a terminal when it interacts with a coherent event.

Axiom 9A1 — *state modulation and coherent determinism* — affirms that coherent events traversing a terminal are *deterministic in geometry* (the basic script of their form is given), but *indeterminate in phenomenological expression* (the way the terminal will live them depends on its evolutionary position). Said simply: *one cannot avoid the coherent event, but one chooses who to be while it happens*. It is, in a certain sense, the axiom of *free will of state*: not freedom *from* something, but freedom *within* the something.

We will address Axiom 9A1 in the next chapter. For now, Axiom 9A hands us an important inheritance: perception is not passive reception of the world, it is structural act that depends on who we are. To expand who we are is to expand what we can see. It is what, for those who take the Ordinative Sciences seriously, becomes a programme of work on themselves.
